## What this is

The Daily Lesson is a daily 5-minute editorial learning unit, in 47 languages, served as a single static page per day. It's the opposite of Duolingo: no gamification, no notification spam, no walled garden, no infinite scroll. One concept per day. One practice. Then close the tab.

Every lesson is:

- **5 minutes** end-to-end (read + recall check)
- **Editorial**, not drill — written, not generated on-the-fly
- **Indexable** as a static page (one URL per lesson, one lesson per day)
- **Open** under CC-BY-4.0 — copy it, fork it, translate it, teach with it
- **Multilingual** — 47 languages from day one (Spanish, French, German, Mandarin, Tagalog, Swahili, Vietnamese, and 40 more)
- **Backed by a real cognitive-science pilot** — pre/post assessment with effect sizes published in public at https://thedailylesson.com/pilot

## Sample lesson — Day 1

A look at one full lesson, structure included. (See `examples/day-001-en.md` for the rendered version.)

> **Day 1 — The word "fluency" doesn't mean what you think it means**
>
> *Hook (30s):* Most people think "fluent" means knowing every word. Native speakers don't know every word in their own language — and yet they're called fluent. So what's the actual definition?
>
> *Story (90s):* In linguistics, fluency is measured by *reading rate*, *speech rate*, and *recovery from error* — not vocabulary size. A 4-year-old native speaker is fluent. They have a 5,000-word vocabulary. You probably know 5,000 words in your target language already.
>
> *Wonder (60s):* What if the bottleneck isn't *words* — it's *recovery from error*?
>
> *Action (60s):* Today, in your target language: say one sentence out loud. Get one word wrong. Then say the next sentence anyway. That's the practice. Recovery, not perfection.
>
> *Wisdom (30s):* "Fluency is not the absence of error. It's the recovery from error."

That's it. 5 minutes. One concept. One practice. Then close the tab.
