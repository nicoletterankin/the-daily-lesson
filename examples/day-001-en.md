# Day 1 — The word "fluency" doesn't mean what you think it means

## Hook (30 seconds)

Most people think being "fluent" in a language means knowing every word.

But here's the catch: native speakers don't know every word in their own language either. The average native English speaker knows about 20,000–35,000 word families. There are over 600,000 words in the Oxford English Dictionary. Native speakers know maybe 3% of their own language. And yet — they're called fluent.

So what does fluency actually mean?

## Story (90 seconds)

In linguistics, fluency is measured by three things:

1. **Reading rate** — how fast you can read a text out loud
2. **Speech rate** — how fast you can speak without unnatural pauses
3. **Recovery from error** — how quickly you can keep going after you say something wrong

Notice what's not on the list: vocabulary size.

A 4-year-old native speaker is fluent. They have a vocabulary of about 5,000 words. You probably already know 5,000 words in your target language — or you're closer to it than you think. So why don't you feel fluent?

It's not the words. It's the recovery.

When a 4-year-old says the wrong word, they don't stop. They don't apologize. They don't open Google Translate. They just keep going. That's the actual skill of fluency: the ability to fail in real time and not lose your place.

## Wonder (60 seconds)

Stop and ask yourself: when you speak your target language, what makes you stop?

It's almost never that you don't know a word. It's that you're afraid you'll get a word wrong. The word is in your head. You just refuse to risk it.

What would happen if, today, you said the wrong word on purpose — and then said the next sentence anyway?

## Action (60 seconds)

Today's practice is one minute long.

1. Pick one sentence you've been meaning to say in your target language. (Anything. "I want a coffee." "Where is the train?" "I am studying right now.")
2. Say it out loud. Get one word wrong on purpose.
3. Without stopping, say the *next* sentence. Anything. Even unrelated.

That's it. The point is to feel what it's like to keep going through a wrong word. The actual feeling. Not the theory.

If you want extra credit: do this with a friend or a stranger today. The whole skill is "I made an error and the world did not end."

## Wisdom (30 seconds)

> Fluency is not the absence of error.
> It is the recovery from error.
