# Curious Kelly — Sales Pitch

*Lesson of the Day, PBC*

---

## The one-line version

Every human on Earth, same day, same lesson — taught live by an AI teacher who can see what you're looking at, respond when you interrupt, and run forever from a single GPU under your desk.

## What it actually is

Land at **kellyai.com/learn**. Within three seconds, Kelly is there — a real-time AI teacher streaming today's 7-minute lesson over WebRTC. You can speak. You can interrupt. You can touch the diagram floating between you and her. You can scrub the timeline, answer the reflection prompt, ask the question you actually have. Kelly's gaze and gestures are choreographed to the visual aids on the shared canvas — she's not a talking head playing over a slide deck, she's a teacher pointing at the thing she's teaching.

The lesson is the same one every other learner on Earth gets that day. Day 1 is January 1. Two tracks: **Learn** (the 365 most important things to know, deepening every year) and **Grow** (AI fluency for the next decade). Twenty-five languages at launch. **461,040 lessons** in the curriculum library, written, not generated at runtime.

Then there's **The Studio** at kellyai.com/studio. Record your face once. Write a lesson script. Compose interactive aids on a unified canvas. Publish a deployable interactive lesson at your own URL. The bar Susan Wojcicki named for interactive video to proliferate as a medium — recording one as easily as recording a normal video — gets cleared.

Both products launch publicly on the same day. The first stranger who arrives sees the category-defining version, not a preview.

## Why it's defensible

**The curriculum is the moat.** Khan, Duolingo, Coursera have courses. We have a daily global lesson — calendar-locked, deepening yearly, 25 languages, 461K lessons already assembled. That predates the AI work and is not reproducible by an LLM at runtime.

**The infrastructure is sovereign.** Every layer of the streaming runtime — speech-to-text, language model, text-to-speech, lip-sync, video composition — runs locally on an owned RTX 5090 under permissive open-source licenses (MIT, Apache 2.0). No metered vendor anywhere in the hot path. Marginal cost per streamed user-minute: **approximately $0**.

Every other AI-avatar education platform — D-ID, Hedra, Tavus, Synthesia, bitHuman — rents its avatar layer from a SaaS vendor at 2–10 credits per minute. Their gross margins compress as they grow. Ours expand. We can afford to make the daily lesson free for every human on Earth, forever. They can't.

**It's kill-switch resistant.** A government that flips its country's internet kill switch breaks every cloud-API-dependent product overnight. Ours keeps working from a USB stick if it has to. Not marketing — what the local stack literally enables.
